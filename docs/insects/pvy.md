## Aphid Vectors of Potato Virus Y

Potato virus Y (PVY) is spread from plant to plant by winged aphids probing leaves as they move through a field. Most PVY is spread by species that don't colonize potato, such as the soybean aphid, which only pass through the field; systemic insecticides don't stop them from transmitting the virus. Colonizing aphids, such as the green peach aphid, settle and reproduce on potato.

### Model details

Degree days are counted from January 1 by the single-sine method, base 39.2°F (4°C), cutoff 86°F. The curve comes from Wisconsin's suction traps, 2016–2023. Each week's catch was scored by how many of each aphid species were caught and how well that species transmits PVY, and a smooth curve was fitted to those scores by degree days, allowing for differences between years and traps (Bradford and Groves, UW–Madison Vegetable Entomology, unpublished analysis, refitted 2026).

- Risk is already Moderate by about 730 degree days (late May in southern Wisconsin, early June in the north), holds near 40 % of the peak through early summer, and turns High at about 2,240 (mid-July in the south, about August 1 near Antigo).
- It stays near its peak from about 2,980 to 3,990 degree days, peaking near 3,580: late August in southern Wisconsin and late September in the northern seed areas, so in the north risk is still rising through the season.
- Before about 290 degree days the traps have no data, and the model shows no risk.

### How to read it

Risk levels compare the day's value with the season's peak: Low from 5 % of the peak, Moderate from 25 %, High from 50 %, Very high from 75 %. The early-season rise is the model's most useful signal: on years left out of the fit, degree days timed the start of the season's catch about two days better than the calendar alone. Later in the season they did no better than the calendar, since most of these aphids fly in from elsewhere, on weather far from your field, so read midsummer and late-season levels as typical pressure for the time of year. Seasons differ widely: in single years the peak has come anywhere from about 1,700 to 4,800 degree days.

For seed potatoes: after early-season seed or at-plant protection wears off, scout weekly for colonies of wingless aphids and treat with systemic foliar aphicides when they appear, rotating modes of action. Paraffinic oils, which interfere with transmission, are applied weekly from about two weeks after full emergence. As risk rises toward High, keep spray coverage up. In the north, risk is still rising when the season ends, so killing vines early, before the late-season surge of aphids, is one of the most effective ways to keep PVY out of a seed lot.

### Limits

The curve describes the trap catches it was fitted to. Base temperatures from 32 to 43°F fitted the catches equally well. The trap records include weeks with aphids but not weeks with none, so the start and end of the season may show more risk than they should.
